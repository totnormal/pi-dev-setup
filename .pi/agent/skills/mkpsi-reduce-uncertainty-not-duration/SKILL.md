# Improve experience by reducing uncertainty rather than objective time.

## R — Reading

> “We would rather wait eight minutes for a train knowing it will arrive in eight minutes, than spend four minutes waiting for a train in a state of anxious uncertainty.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Complaints framed as speed/capacity may actually be variance, uncertainty, control, or wasted waiting.
  - **Intervene:** Add countdowns, guarantees, result-time promises, flexible boarding, route variance choices, or status visibility.
  - **Assess:** Measure satisfaction, abandonment, anxiety, and perceived journey length alongside objective time.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. London Underground dot-matrix displays increased passenger satisfaction without faster trains.
  2. Men’s medical testing: a 24-hour result promise might raise uptake by reducing dreaded uncertainty.
- **Trigger phrases:** “We must make it faster”; “The average time is acceptable”; “They just need to wait”; “The delay is only a few minutes.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not operational speed optimization; sometimes slower-but-certain wins.
  - Not transparency theater; information must be accurate enough to create trust.
- **Boundaries:** Do not use reassuring displays to hide dangerous delays or false certainty; communicate uncertainty honestly when needed.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Add accurate wait/status information and compare satisfaction at same objective duration.
  - **V2:** Segment by context: airport vs. leisure route, medical testing vs. casual browsing.
  - **V3:** Optimize for “least-bad worst case” and variance, not only average time.”
