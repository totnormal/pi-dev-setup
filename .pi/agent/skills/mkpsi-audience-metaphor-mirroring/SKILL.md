# Mirror the audience’s own metaphor to create tribal safety.

## R — Reading

> “If you want to win people over, listen to their metaphors. This gives you an insight into their world-view: an insight that can help you to win them over.”
- **Atomic executable skill:** In discovery/listening, capture the audience’s repeated metaphors, then respond inside the same frame for the opening offer or recommendation.
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Stakeholder uses a repeated frame: unlocking, journey, science, family, battle, nature, craft.
  - **Intervene:** Echo the frame in one or two key phrases: “If the problem is locked, here is the key…”
  - **Assess:** Audience shows less recoil and more “you get us” language.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. HR director says “unlock potential”; Lancaster answers with the “key” from his workshops.
  2. Using “shoot down this perception” would have signaled a more aggressive tribe and weakened fit.
- **Trigger phrases:** “Unlock”; “path forward”; “engine room”; “family”; “ecosystem”; “scientific approach”; “game plan.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:** Not mimicry of jargon for its own sake; not metaphor invention from the speaker’s preferences.
- **Boundaries:** Mirror only ethical, non-harmful frames; if the audience metaphor is destructive, bridge gently to a safer adjacent frame.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Document audience frame before drafting.
  - **V2:** Check whether the first recommendation uses the same frame.
  - **V3:** Track stakeholder trust and speed to agreement.”
