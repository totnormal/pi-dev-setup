# Define the Perfect World Purchase Criteria

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> The purpose of this step in the sales pitch is to clearly state what you believe the purchase criteria should be for a solution, assuming the buyer is a best-fit customer and they agree with your point of view.
>
> — April Dunford, Step 3: The Perfect World

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Perfect World is the bridge from market education to your product. After discussing alternatives, state what an ideal solution should provide for a buyer like them. If the buyer agrees, they have accepted criteria that map to your differentiated value. If they disagree, they may not be a fit.

---

## A1 — Application in the Book (Past Application)

For agency project management, the perfect world is a tool as easy as Trello, designed for multi-project resource management, without enterprise complexity. Agreement with this frame sets up the specialist product as the obvious next step.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - Summarize what the alternatives discussion revealed about trade-offs.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

2. **Step 2**
   - Write 2–4 criteria for an ideal solution that preserve upsides and avoid major downsides.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

3. **Step 3**
   - Phrase the criteria in buyer language, not product jargon.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

4. **Ask for explicit alignment**
   - Ask for explicit alignment: “Does that match what you need?”
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

5. **Verification layer**
   - Verification layer: check that every criterion maps to differentiated value you can actually deliver; remove criteria where competitors are equally strong.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not manipulate criteria dishonestly.
- If the prospect rejects the criteria, qualify out or revisit discovery.
- Do not spend too long here; it is a transition to value.

### Common failure modes from Dunford's books

- Treating positioning as copywriting rather than strategic context.
- Talking about features before clarifying alternatives and value.
- Using generic trends, generic problems, or generic proof.
- Letting the buyer infer the purchase criteria alone.
