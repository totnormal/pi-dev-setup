# Finance growth by sequencing risk against real sell-through evidence.

## R — Reading

> “‘Life is growth,’ I said. ‘Business is growth. You grow or you die.’”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Demand is real but cash, banks, suppliers, or balance-sheet norms are choking growth.
  - **Intervene:** Separate survival risk from growth risk: keep a fallback income, reinvest surplus, ask for larger credit only after sell-through, and search for financiers whose model rewards upside.
  - **Assess:** Measure order cycle payback, supplier reliability, credit dependence, and whether each financing source matches the growth thesis.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Knight repeatedly doubles Onitsuka orders, sells out, repays loans, then returns to the bank for larger letters of credit.
  2. When First National and Wallace become fatal constraints, he explores Nissho Iwai because trading companies understand high-volume, low-margin growth.
- **Trigger phrases:** “We’re growing too fast for our balance sheet”; “The bank says slow down”; “Demand exceeds cash”; “No venture capital exists for this.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not reckless leverage; growth must be anchored in sell-through and repayment evidence.
  - Not anti-accounting; Knight’s accounting work also taught him why lack of equity kills companies.
- **Boundaries:** Do not use if unit economics are unproven, liabilities are hidden, or creditors/investors are misled.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Build a cash conversion map for one order cycle: deposit, production, shipping, sell-through, repayment.
  - **V2:** Add at least two non-identical financing options before the primary lender becomes existential.
  - **V3:** Institutionalize financing strategy around growth partners, covenant safety, and supplier diversification.”
