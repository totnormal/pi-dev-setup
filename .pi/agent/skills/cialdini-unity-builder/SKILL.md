# Unity Builder

## R — Reading

> “The relationships that lead people to favor another most effectively are not those that allow them to say, ‘Oh, that person is like us’… but ‘of us.’”
>
> — Robert B. Cialdini, Chapters 11–12: Unity

---

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

1. Unity is deeper than liking: it makes the persuader and audience part of the same perceived self or group.
2. It can be based on shared identity (“being together”) or synchronized/cooperative action (“acting together”).
3. Ethically, unity should reveal or build a real shared purpose, not invent kinship to extract compliance.
4. Use it for communities, teams, cohorts, rituals, and movements where mutual obligation is appropriate.

---

## A1 — Application in the source

### Case 1: Holiday cards to strangers revisited
- **Problem**: Cialdini first treated returned cards as reciprocity, then reframed relationship itself as a source of assent.
- **Methodology use**: Perceived social connection created favorable response.
- **Result**: Relationship can be an independent influence context.

### Case 2: Synchronized Polish rituals / collective action
- **Problem**: Marching, singing, chanting, or acting in unison created powerful “all for one” feelings.
- **Methodology use**: Acting together can create unity.
- **Result**: Rituals should serve real collective purpose, not blind conformity.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not fabricate intimacy, kinship, insider status, or shared identity.
- Do not use unity to create out-group hostility or suppress dissent.
- Avoid high-pressure rituals that make refusal socially costly.

### Global Cialdini safety constraints

- Truthful evidence beats persuasion technique; if the evidence is weak, say so.
- Preserve meaningful choice; avoid hidden defaults, fake constraints, or social pressure that makes refusal unsafe.
- Name the influence mechanism in the answer so the user can judge it consciously.
