# Sequence persuasion through trust, care, and apparent logic.

## R — Reading

> “Ethos means credibility, pathos means emotion and logos means logic, or the appearance of logic… ‘Can I trust you?’ (ethos); ‘Do I care about what you are saying?’ (pathos); and ‘Are you right, or do you sound right?’ (logos).”
- **Atomic executable skill:** Before drafting a speech, pitch, or difficult message, write one sentence each for: (1) why the audience can trust you, (2) why this matters emotionally to them, (3) why the claim sounds coherent and grounded.
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Message is over-indexed on facts, slides, or “business case” and under-indexed on trust/emotion.
  - **Intervene:** Draft in order: credibility cue → emotional relevance → logical proof/pattern.
  - **Assess:** A listener can answer “I trust you / I care / this sounds right” without prompting.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Aristotle’s ethos/pathos/logos mapped to “Can I trust you? Do I care? Are you right?”
  2. Lancaster’s APET model: communication should start with instinct, move to emotion, and finish with logic.
- **Trigger phrases:** “The facts speak for themselves”; “Let me walk you through the analysis”; “Why aren’t people buying this?”; “We need a more compelling leadership message.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:** Not the same as a generic three-part structure; not the same as storytelling; not pure logical argumentation.
- **Boundaries:** Do not fabricate credibility or emotional stakes; if the logic is genuinely weak, fix the argument before polishing the language.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Annotate draft with E/P/L labels.
  - **V2:** Test with a small audience: ask which of trust/care/logic felt weakest.
  - **V3:** Measure downstream persuasion: support, recall, action, or reduced objections.”

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

- **Recognize:** Message is over-indexed on facts, slides, or “business case” and under-indexed on trust/emotion.
  - **Intervene:** Draft in order: credibility cue → emotional relevance → logical proof/pattern.
  - **Assess:** A listener can answer “I trust you / I care / this sounds right” without prompting.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Aristotle’s ethos/pathos/logos mapped to “Can I trust you? Do I care? Are you right?”
  2. Lancaster’s APET model: communication should start with instinct, move to emotion, and finish with logic.
- **Trigger phrases:** “The facts speak for themselves”; “Let me walk you through the analysis”; “Why aren’t people buying this?”; “We need a more compelling leadership message.”

  - **V2:** Test with a small audience: ask which of trust/care/logic felt weakest.
  - **V3:** Measure downstream persuasion: support, recall, action, or reduced objections.
