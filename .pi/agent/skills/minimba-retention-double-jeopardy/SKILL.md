# evaluating retention/defection ROI

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Customer defection rates follow double jeopardy — a brand's defection rate is essentially a function of its market share and category. Asking a brand to halve its defection rate is asking it to increase its customer base thirtyfold.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010), Ch 3 How To Grow Your Customer Base; Ch 13 The Scientific Laws

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Retention Double Jeopardy: larger brands have slightly lower defection rates (as a % of their base), smaller brands slightly higher — but all brands in a category have similar defection rates. This means the Reichheld/Sasser claim ("retain 5% more customers = 100% profit boost") is fantasy: it's a thought experiment that assumed zero cost to halve defection. In reality, defection is structural and can't be radically altered without massive share gain. The sales potential of retention is ~50x smaller than the potential of acquisition.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

US car brands: defection rates 58-72% (avg 67%), regardless of brand. UK car brands: 31-57% (avg 49%). No brand achieves dramatically lower defection without dramatically higher share. Sharp's math: Toyota could gain max 1 share point from zero defection, but 50 share points are up for grabs from acquisition each year.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - Obtain defection/churn rates for your brand and 2-3 competitors over the same period.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

2. **Step 2**
   - Compare to the category average and the double-jeopardy prediction for your share. Expect similarity, not dramatic differences.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

3. **Step 3**
   - Calculate the maximum upside from zero defection (often <2 share points) vs the upside from acquisition (often 20-50x larger).
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

4. **Step 4**
   - Challenge any ROI claim that assumes easy defection reduction — benchmark against what no brand has achieved.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

5. **Step 5**
   - Balance budget: invest proportionally to the opportunity. Acquisition deserves far more than retention.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Subscription services with contractual switching barriers CAN achieve lower churn. B2B relationships may differ. But even there, defection follows predictable patterns. Do not use this to ignore genuine service failures — fix the causes of unnecessary churn, but don't expect to change the structural defection rate.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
