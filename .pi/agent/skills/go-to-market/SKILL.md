# Section 1: GTM Strategy & Motion Selection

## Choose Your GTM Motion

| Motion | Best for | ACV | Cycle | Key Metric |
|--------|----------|-----|-------|-----------|
| **Product-Led Growth (PLG)** | Self-serve tools, developer products | <$1K | Days | Free→paid conversion |
| **Sales-Assisted PLG** | Mid-market, product-led but needs human touch | $1K-$10K | Weeks | PQL→meeting rate |
| **Sales-Led (SMB)** | Non-technical buyers, clear ROI | $5K-$25K | 1-3 months | Lead→SQL conversion |
| **Enterprise** | Complex solutions, compliance needs | $50K+ | 3-12 months | Pipeline coverage |

## PLG Motion
- Product must deliver value before asking for payment
- Viral loops: collaboration, sharing, referrals
- Usage-based or freemium pricing
- Activation metric: the action that correlates with retention
- In-app prompts for upgrade moments

## Sales-Led Motion
- Clear ICP and target account list
- Outbound prospecting + inbound lead capture
- Demo-driven sales process
- Marketing-generated awareness → sales closes
- Requires sales enablement collateral

## Hybrid/Multi-Motion
- Start with one motion, add others as you scale
- Common path: PLG → PQLs → sales team for expansion
- Don't mix motions within the same team/ICP initially

# Section 2: ICP & Positioning

## Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

### Firmographic Criteria
- Industry / vertical
- Company size (employees, revenue)
- Geographic location
- Tech stack / tools used
- Funding stage (for startups)

### Buyer Persona
- Role / title
- Responsibilities
- Pain points they're measured on
- Where they get information
- Buying authority (economic buyer vs. influencer vs. end user)

### Anti-ICP (who NOT to target)
- Too small / too large
- Wrong tech stack
- Wrong industry
- Geographic constraints
- Already solved (incumbent too sticky)

## Positioning Framework

### The Positioning Statement
```
For [target customer] who [statement of need or opportunity],
[product name] is a [product category] that [key benefit / reason to buy].
Unlike [primary competitive alternative], our product [most important differentiator].
```

### Value Proposition Canvas
1. **Customer Jobs**: What are they trying to accomplish?
2. **Pains**: What frustrates them about current solutions?
3. **Gains**: What would make them happy/excited?
4. **Your Product**: How does it address each pain and create each gain?

### Differentiation Types
- **Performance**: Faster, better, cheaper
- **Category**: New category (you define the game)
- **Audience**: Built specifically for X (narrow focus)
- **Channel**: Distributed differently (viral, community, PLG)
- **Model**: Different pricing (usage-based, open source)

# Section 3: Product Launch Planning

## Launch Checklist

### Pre-Launch (4-8 weeks out)
- [ ] ICP and positioning defined
- [ ] Competitive analysis complete
- [ ] Pricing and packaging finalized
- [ ] Landing page ready (collect waitlist/emails)
- [ ] Beta customers identified and onboarded
- [ ] Product marketing context document created

### Launch Week
- [ ] Press release / announcement ready
- [ ] Product Hunt launch (if applicable)
- [ ] Social media posts prepared (Twitter, LinkedIn)
- [ ] Email to existing contacts/customers
- [ ] Blog post announcing the launch
- [ ] Demo video / walkthrough recorded
- [ ] Sales team briefed with battle cards and talking points

### Post-Launch (1-4 weeks)
- [ ] Monitor feedback and respond quickly
- [ ] Track key metrics (signups, activation, paying customers)
- [ ] Collect customer testimonials
- [ ] Iterate based on feedback
- [ ] Plan v2 features based on usage data

## Launch Channels by Product Type

| Product | Primary Channels | Secondary Channels |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Developer tool | Hacker News, GitHub, dev communities | Twitter, product blogs |
| B2B SaaS | Product Hunt, LinkedIn, industry blogs | Newsletter sponsorships, webinars |
| Consumer app | Social media (TikTok, IG), word-of-mouth | PR, influencer partnerships |
| Enterprise | Sales outreach, industry events, analyst briefings | Case studies, white papers |

# Section 5: Product-Market Fit Validation

## Signs You Have PMF
- Organic growth (word-of-mouth, referrals increasing)
- Retention curves flattening (users who hit month 3 stay for 12+)
- Sean Ellis test: 40%+ of users say they'd be "very disappointed" without it
- Customers actively asking for features (engagement, not just usage)
- Sales cycle shortening

## Signs You Don't Have PMF Yet
- High churn after initial signup
- Sales team struggling to close (long cycles, high objection rate)
- Marketing generates leads but poor conversion
- Growth requires heavy paid acquisition without organic pull
- Low feature adoption beyond core functionality

## PMF Diagnostic Questions
1. How are users finding you today? (organic vs. paid ratio)
2. What's month-3 retention? (<30% = no PMF, >50% = approaching PMF)
3. Are customers expanding usage? (seats, features, spend)
4. What's the NPS? (>30 = approaching PMF, >50 = strong PMF signal)
5. Would they actively recommend you? (net promoter behavior)
