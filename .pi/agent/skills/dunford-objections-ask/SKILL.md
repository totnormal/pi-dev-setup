# Handle Unspoken Objections and Make the Ask

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Just like the other stages of the sales pitch, you want to be deliberate about the way your conversation comes to an end.
>
> — April Dunford, Step 7: Objections; Step 8: The Ask

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Buyers often fear deployment, adoption, integration, cost, or stakeholder resistance even when they like the value. Address critical predictable objections proactively if you have good answers. Then guide the buyer to the simplest next step in the purchase process. A good ask helps good-fit buyers move forward and lets bad-fit buyers exit early.

---

## A1 — Application in the Book (Past Application)

Dunford gives examples of addressing deployment timelines, training, modular rollout, security certifications, and integrations before recommending a proof of concept, custom demo, trial, quote, or direct purchase path.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Skip objection handling when no critical objection needs proactive treatment.
- Do not surface objections you cannot answer.
- Do not treat every prospect as worth advancing.

### Common failure modes from Dunford's books

- Treating positioning as copywriting rather than strategic context.
- Talking about features before clarifying alternatives and value.
- Using generic trends, generic problems, or generic proof.
- Letting the buyer infer the purchase criteria alone.
