# Loyalty Program Evidence

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Loyalty programs produce very slight loyalty effects, and do practically nothing to drive growth. The consequent effect on profits is presumably negative.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010), Ch 11 Why Loyalty Programs Don't Work

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Loyalty programs are self-selecting: they recruit existing heavy, loyal buyers (who have more to gain) and fail to reach light/non-buyers. They reward people for behavior they would exhibit anyway. The weak loyalty effect is inconsistent across brands and often swamped by other marketing activities. Programs are hard to exit due to sunk costs, consumer expectations, and legal constraints — so firms remain "loyal to their loyalty programs" far longer than rational economics would recommend.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

The Ehrenberg-Bass Institute's study of FlyBuys (one of the world's largest cross-category programs) showed loyalty effects so weak they were inconsistent across brands. Meyer-Waarden & Benavent replicated this in French supermarkets; Leenheer et al. in Dutch supermarkets; all found tiny effects. Programs should make brands look "niche" (high loyalty for their share) but they don't — penetration barely shifts.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - Benchmark: if the program works, the brand should show unusually high loyalty for its market share (niche pattern). Check if it does.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

2. **Step 2**
   - Control for selection effects: compare members vs matched non-members, not vs average non-members (who are lighter buyers by default).
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

3. **Step 3**
   - Assess reach: what % of category buyers does the program reach? (Most programs only reach existing brand buyers.)
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

4. **Step 4**
   - Calculate net cost: program costs + rewards given to already-loyal buyers, minus any incremental sales.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

5. **Step 5**
   - If ROI is negative, plan exit strategy: honour existing points, sunset gracefully, redirect budget to reach/penetration.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

6. **Step 6**
   - If the program has data-collection value (customer insight), weigh that against the loyalty-building justification.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Some loyalty programs have secondary value: data collection, CRM infrastructure, retailer relationships. The evidence says they don't build loyalty or drive growth — but that doesn't mean ALL program spending is wasted if those secondary benefits are real. B2B and subscription contexts may differ from consumer FMCG.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
