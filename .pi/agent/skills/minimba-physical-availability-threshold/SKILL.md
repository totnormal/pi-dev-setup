# setting distribution targets

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> 80% or more weighted physical distribution is necessary, but not sufficient, for a brand to have high market share.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010); Marketing TEP — Byron Sharp, Ch 12 Seven Rules; Ch 13 The Scientific Laws

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Physical availability has a non-linear relationship with market share. Below ~80% weighted distribution, a brand cannot achieve high share no matter how good its advertising. Above 80%, more distribution helps but isn't sufficient — mental availability and product quality also matter. This threshold effect means distribution is the first growth lever to pull: if you're below 80%, advertising spend is largely wasted. Once above 80%, the growth battle shifts to mental availability.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Sharp notes that brands stuck below 80% distribution face a structural ceiling on share. The "reasons not to buy" rule (Ch 12) includes being unavailable when the buyer wants to purchase. Excessive pricing encourages retailers to launch private-label competitors, reducing effective distribution.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - Measure weighted physical distribution (% of category sales through outlets stocking your brand, weighted by outlet volume).
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

2. **Step 2**
   - If below 80%: prioritize distribution expansion over advertising. Every percentage point of distribution is worth more than advertising spend at this stage.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

3. **Step 3**
   - If above 80%: shift investment to mental availability (advertising, distinctive assets). Distribution has diminishing returns above the threshold.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

4. **Step 4**
   - Check for "reasons not to buy" that reduce effective availability: delisting risk, out-of-stocks, private-label displacement, excessive pricing.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

5. **Step 5**
   - Set share targets that are realistic for your distribution level. A brand at 50% distribution cannot be a share leader.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Digital/DTC brands operate differently — "distribution" includes search ranking, app store visibility, platform presence. The threshold concept still applies (you need sufficient "digital shelf space") but the 80% metric is physical-retail-specific. Services and B2B have different availability dynamics.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
