# a targeting strategy assumes a unique customer base

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> The good news is that your customers are just like your competitors' customers, and their customers are like yours. Competing brands sell to the same sort of people.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010); Marketing TEP — Byron Sharp, Ch 5 Our Buyers Are Different; Ch 13 The Scientific Laws

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Across hundreds of brands in dozens of categories, rival brands have near-identical customer profiles — demographics, psychographics, values, and media habits all deviate by only 2-3 percentage points from the category norm. Segmentation studies amplify tiny random differences into illusory "segments." Targeting strategies that assume a unique customer base are fighting against reality: the bigger you get, the more your customer base resembles the population.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Evans (1959) gave personality tests to Ford and Chevrolet owners — profiles were identical. Kennedy & Ehrenberg (2000) profiled hundreds of brands across 40+ categories using demographics, psychographics, attitudes, values, media habits — average deviation was 2 percentage points. Even overtly targeted brands (e.g. a women's brand) end up with normal customer bases once they gain meaningful share.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - Pull the demographic/psychographic profile of your brand's buyers and 2-3 competitors' buyers.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

2. **Step 2**
   - Calculate the deviation from the category average for each variable. Expect 2-3 percentage points, not 20.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

3. **Step 3**
   - Flag any variable with >5pp deviation — these are usually obvious (price tier, physical size) not "psychographic segments."
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

4. **Step 4**
   - Challenge the segmentation study: if real segments existed, brands would have measurably different customer bases. They don't.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

5. **Step 5**
   - Redirect targeting budget to mass reach — your buyers are everyone's buyers.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Premium/luxury brands DO have different user profiles (wealthier buyers). Niche brands in early growth may temporarily skew. But as share grows, the customer base normalizes. Do not use this to justify ignoring genuine demographic differences in B2B or highly specialized markets.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
