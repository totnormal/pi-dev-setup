# Price Promotion Evidence

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Price promotions have an immediate and positive effect on sales. But the effect does not last... they lack reach and usually fail to bring new customers to a brand.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010), Ch 10 What Price Promotions Really Do

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Price promotions mostly reward existing light buyers who happen to find the brand on deal. They do not alter underlying purchase propensities — after the promotion, buyers resume their previous behavior. There is no "purchase reinforcement effect." Promotions are addictive: once you rely on them to hit volume targets, cutting back means missing targets. They also empower retailers and crowd out innovation investment.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Ehrenberg, Hammond & Goodhardt found almost everyone who bought a brand during a promotion had bought it previously. Some sales are pulled forward from future periods, partially cancelling the spike. Consumers buy across price tiers in line with market share — there is no dedicated "deal-prone" segment. Pauwels et al. found more emphasis on promotions accompanies less emphasis on innovation.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

These findings apply to established brands in competitive FMCG/retail. New products may benefit from trial-generating promotions. Promotions CAN be rational when used strategically (clearing inventory, meeting retailer demands, competitive response) — the danger is when they are mistaken for a growth strategy.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
