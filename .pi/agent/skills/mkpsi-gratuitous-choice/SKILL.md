# Add a painless choice to create agency and liking.

## R — Reading

> “People seem to like choice for its own sake.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Users resent a monopoly, mandate, default, or single path even when the service is objectively good.
  - **Intervene:** Add a small, low-cognitive-load choice in channel, courier, timing, contribution target, display, or route.
  - **Assess:** Track response rate, blame allocation, satisfaction, and perceived control.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. British Telecom: response by phone only got 2.9%, post only 5%, but phone-or-post choice got 7.8%.
  2. Online retailers rarely offer courier choice, though Sutherland argues it would increase preference and reduce blame for delivery failure.
- **Trigger phrases:** “Choice would be inefficient”; “We should drive everyone to one channel”; “It’s objectively the same service”; “Why do they dislike a good monopoly?”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not maximal choice; too much choice creates paralysis.
  - Not personalization necessarily; even gratuitous low-stakes choice can work.
- **Boundaries:** Keep choices mentally painless and honest; avoid dark-pattern choices or hiding the best option.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Add one binary/limited choice and compare conversion/satisfaction.
  - **V2:** Test whether choice improves perceived fairness and reduces blame.
  - **V3:** Build agency moments into services without fragmenting operations.”
