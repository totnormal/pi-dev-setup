# Add a credible cost that proves commitment.

## R — Reading

> “Information is free, but sincerity is not.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Customers doubt whether a promise is real because words are cheap.
  - **Intervene:** Add visible cost: effort, expense, risk, craftsmanship, inconvenience, time, bravery, or foregone short-term profit.
  - **Assess:** Test whether observers infer quality, commitment, openness, or trust before they consciously articulate why.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Coffee shop chairs: moveable outdoor furniture signaled the shop was open, cared-for, invested in, and likely not terrible.
  2. Wedding invitations/handmade cards: effort, creativity, stamp, and production value convey care more credibly than a factual email.
- **Trigger phrases:** “Can’t we just say we care?”; “This is inefficient”; “Why spend on packaging/furniture/ritual?”; “The sign already says open.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not luxury aesthetics by default; the cost must map to the intended inference.
  - Not waste for waste’s sake; costly signals can backfire if they imply high price or vanity.
- **Boundaries:** Avoid costly signals that create safety risks, environmental harm, exclusion, or false expectations.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Ask what inference the cost creates; test against cheap-talk control.
  - **V2:** Calibrate the amount and type of cost to avoid “too expensive” or “performative” readings.
  - **V3:** Link signals to repeat exchange/reputation so cheating is punishable.”
