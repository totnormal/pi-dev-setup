# Trust Stack: Liking + Authority + Honest Weakness

## R — Reading

> “We most prefer to say yes to the requests of someone we know and like.”
>
> — Robert B. Cialdini, Influence ch.5–6; Pre-Suasion ch.10

---

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

1. Trust is built from two related routes: liking (similarity, sincere praise, cooperation, positive association) and authority (expertise, credentials, confidence, relevant symbols).
2. Cialdini’s stronger modern move is to add honest weakness before strengths: a real limitation can make subsequent claims more credible.
3. The stack must match evidence to claim: likability cannot replace competence, and credentials cannot replace relevance.
4. Use the minimum proof needed to make the audience comfortable acting.

---

## A1 — Application in the source

### Case 1: Tupperware party
- **Problem**: A friendly host, not a remote salesperson, drove purchases.
- **Methodology use**: Liking and social ties made the request easier to accept.
- **Result**: Trust often travels through relationships.

### Case 2: Titles/clothes/automobiles authority studies
- **Problem**: People over-obeyed symbols of authority.
- **Methodology use**: Authority cues can trigger deference even when shallow.
- **Result**: Final skill must verify substance behind symbols.

### Case 3: Avis “We’re number two”
- **Problem**: Admitting a weakness made the brand’s “we try harder” claim credible.
- **Methodology use**: Weakness-first sequencing increased trust.
- **Result**: Use only real, non-fatal weaknesses.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not fake similarity, praise, credentials, titles, testimonials, or authority symbols.
- Do not use attractive design or confident tone to compensate for missing evidence.
- Do not disclose weaknesses that are material blockers without solving them.

### Global Cialdini safety constraints

- Truthful evidence beats persuasion technique; if the evidence is weak, say so.
- Preserve meaningful choice; avoid hidden defaults, fake constraints, or social pressure that makes refusal unsafe.
- Name the influence mechanism in the answer so the user can judge it consciously.
