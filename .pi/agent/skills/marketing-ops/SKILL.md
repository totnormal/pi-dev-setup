# Section 1: Content Marketing

## Content Strategy Pillars

### The Content Pyramid
- **Pillar content** (1-2 pieces/month): Comprehensive guides, original research, definitive resources
- **Cluster content** (4-8 pieces/month): Supporting articles that link to pillars
- **Distribution content** (weekly): Social posts, email newsletters, short-form content

### Content Types by Funnel Stage

| Stage | Content Type | Goal | Measure |
|-------|-------------|------|---------|
| **Top (Awareness)** | Blog posts, infographics, social | Traffic, reach | Page views, social shares |
| **Middle (Consideration)** | Case studies, webinars, comparisons | Lead capture | Email signups, downloads |
| **Bottom (Decision)** | Demos, free trials, ROI calculators | Conversion | Trials started, demos booked |

### Content Calendar Principles
- Plan 4-6 weeks ahead for evergreen content
- Leave 20% flexible for trending topics
- Mix formats: articles, videos, podcasts, infographics
- Each piece should have a clear CTA and distribution plan

### Content Repurposing Framework
One pillar piece → 10+ derivative pieces:
- Pillar article → 5 social posts, 1 email newsletter, 1 infographic, 1 video script, 2 thread posts

# Section 2: Lead Magnets

## Lead Magnet Types

| Type | Best For | Effort | Conversion Rate |
|------|----------|--------|----------------|
| **Checklist/Cheat Sheet** | Quick reference | Low (1-2 hrs) | 20-40% |
| **Template/Spreadsheet** | Practical tool | Medium (3-5 hrs) | 25-45% |
| **Ebook/Guide** | Comprehensive education | High (10-20 hrs) | 15-30% |
| **Mini-course** | Deep education | High (20-40 hrs) | 20-35% |
| **Assessment/Quiz** | Self-discovery | Medium (5-10 hrs) | 30-50% |
| **Free tool/Calculator** | Immediate value | High (20+ hrs) | 25-50% |

## Lead Magnet Design Principles

### Must Be Immediately Useful
- Solve one specific, painful problem
- Deliver value in the first 10 seconds
- No fluff or filler — dense with useful information

### Match the Content It Supports
- Blog post about SEO → SEO audit checklist
- Landing page about productivity → Productivity template
- Webinar about sales → Objection handling script

### Gate vs. Ungate
- **Gated** (email required): For high-value, comprehensive content
- **Ungated** (free access): For awareness-stage content, SEO benefit
- **Hybrid**: Free preview, gated full version

## Lead Magnet Delivery
1. Thank-you page (immediate download or access)
2. Welcome email (deliver content, introduce next step)
3. Nurture sequence (3-7 emails over 2 weeks, see email-outbound)

## Distribution
- Blog post inline CTAs
- Dedicated landing page (SEO-optimized)
- Social media promotion
- Email signature
- Exit-intent popup

# Section 3: Free Tool Marketing

## Why Free Tools Work
- Earn backlinks naturally (other sites link to useful tools)
- Higher conversion rates than content-based lead magnets
- Generate qualified leads (people actively using your tool)
- Build brand authority and trust

## Free Tool Types

| Tool Type | Example | Effort | Value |
|-----------|---------|--------|-------|
| **Calculator** | ROI calculator, pricing estimator | Medium | High perceived value |
| **Grader** | Website grader, content scorer | High | Drives qualified leads |
| **Generator** | Blog title generator, hashtag generator | Medium-High | Shareable, viral potential |
| **Audit tool** | SEO audit, security scan | High | Demonstrates expertise |
| **Template library** | Notion templates, design templates | Low-Medium | High engagement |
| **Comparison tool** | "Which X is right for you?" quiz | Low-Medium | Highly qualified leads |

## Free Tool Best Practices
- Solve real problem, not a demo of your product
- Gated results (email to see full results) is most effective
- Offer upgrade path to paid product naturally
- Collect data about the user's situation for personalization
- Share results as shareable social cards (viral loop)

# Section 4: Marketing Psychology

## Cognitive Biases in Marketing

### Social Proof
- **What**: People follow what others do
- **Apply**: Testimonials, case studies, user numbers, "trending" badges
- **Strongest**: Specific numbers ("12,847 users") beat vague claims ("thousands")

### Scarcity & Urgency
- **What**: People value things that are limited
- **Apply**: Limited-time offers, countdown timers, limited spots
- **Rule**: Must be genuine or it destroys trust

### Anchoring
- **What**: First number seen influences perception
- **Apply**: Show original price before discount, enterprise tier first
- **Example**: "$99/mo (was $149/mo)" — $149 anchors the value

### Loss Aversion
- **What**: People fear loss 2× more than they value gain
- **Apply**: "Don't miss out" > "Get this benefit"
- **Example**: "Stop losing $2,000/month in wasted ad spend"

### Framing Effect
- **What**: How information is presented changes decisions
- **Apply**: "95% success rate" > "5% failure rate"
- **Apply**: "Save $50/month" > "Costs $50/month less"

### Reciprocity
- **What**: People feel obligated to return favors
- **Apply**: Free content, free tools, value-first approach
- **Apply**: Give before asking (content, help, insights)

### Authority
- **What**: People trust experts and recognized brands
- **Apply**: Expert quotes, credentials, media mentions, certifications
- **Apply**: Data and research citations

### Commitment & Consistency
- **What**: People want to be consistent with past actions
- **Apply**: Start with small ask, then escalate
- **Apply**: "You said X was important — here's how we solve it"

## Mental Models for Marketing

### The Hook Model (Nir Eyal)
Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment

### JTBD (Jobs to Be Done)
People don't buy products — they "hire" them to make progress

### The 4Ps (Marketing Mix)
Product, Price, Place, Promotion — see marketing-tactics-4ps

# Section 5: Marketing Ideation

## Idea Generation Frameworks

### "They Ask, You Answer" (Marcus Sheridan)
- Answer every question your customers ask
- Document your process and share it
- Be transparent about pricing, problems, and comparisons

### Content Gap Analysis
1. List 5 main competitor sites
2. Extract their top content topics
3. Cross-reference with your content
4. Topics they cover that you don't = opportunities

### Trend Spotting
- Google Trends (rising queries in your space)
- Reddit threads (what people are discussing)
- Industry newsletters (emerging topics)
- Customer support tickets (unanswered questions)
- Social media comments (unaddressed concerns)

# Section 6: Customer Analysis

## Buyer Persona Development

### Demographics
- Age range, location, income level
- Job title, company size, industry
- Education level, tech proficiency

### Psychographics
- Goals and aspirations
- Fears and frustrations
- Values and beliefs
- Media consumption habits
- Purchase decision criteria

### Behavioral
- How do they research solutions?
- Who influences their decisions?
- What triggers them to look for alternatives?
- What objections do they raise?
- What's their decision timeline?

### Jobs to Be Done Framework
> "What job are they hiring your product to do?"

**Functional Job**: What practical task does it help them complete?
**Emotional Job**: How does it make them feel?
**Social Job**: How does it help them be perceived by others?

**Example (for project management software):**
- Functional: "Keep my team on track and organized"
- Emotional: "Reduce my anxiety about missing deadlines"
- Social: "Look like a competent, organized leader"

## Voice of Customer Research

### Where to Find Customer Language
- **Reviews**: G2, Capterra, Trustpilot (direct customer words)
- **Reddit**: Subreddits in your space (unfiltered opinions)
- **Support tickets**: Most common questions and complaints
- **Sales calls**: Objections, pain points, decision criteria
- **Social media comments**: Questions, complaints, praise
- **Customer interviews**: Deep understanding of motivations

### Pain Point Analysis
1. **Surface pains**: What they say (explicit complaints)
2. **Deep pains**: What causes the surface pain (root cause)
3. **Impact pains**: What happens if unresolved (consequence)
4. **Emotional pains**: How it makes them feel

### Sentiment Analysis
Monitor across channels:
- Brand mentions (positive/negative/neutral ratio)
- Review ratings and trends
- NPS score over time
- Support ticket sentiment
- Social media engagement tone
