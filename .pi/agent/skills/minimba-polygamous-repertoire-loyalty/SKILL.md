# setting loyalty targets

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Loyalty is everywhere but it's seldom exclusive — buyers purchase more than one brand. Polygamous or divided loyalty is quite the norm. No brand should expect its buyers to be 100% loyal.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010), Ch 7 Passionate Customer Commitment

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Loyalty is natural buying behavior (habit, risk-reduction, time-saving), not emotional commitment. Buyers restrict their purchases to a personal repertoire of brands — typically 2-3 — and keep returning to favourites. But 100% loyalty is rare (~13% of a brand's base, and that's inflated by light buyers who only bought once). Loyalty is prosaic and structural (driven by availability, habit, opportunity), not passionate. Expecting or targeting exclusive loyalty is fighting human nature.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Tucker's (1964) bread experiment: women quickly adopted loyal behaviors to identical loaves labeled differently. Car buyers consider only ~2 brands on average. TV viewers with 200 channels watch only ~15/week. Sole buyers average 13% of a brand's base. Categories purchased less frequently show higher "100% loyalty" — an artifact of opportunity, not commitment.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - Calculate the actual % of 100%-loyal buyers in your base over a 12-month period. Expect ~13%, less for frequently-bought categories.
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

2. **Step 2**
   - Segment your buyers by repertoire size: how many brands does the average buyer use?
   - Completion standard: a concrete, inspectable decision, list, artifact, or revision exists.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Subscription/contractual services (telecom, SaaS) can achieve higher exclusivity due to switching costs. But even there, divided attention and multi-provider behavior exists. Do not use this to justify ignoring churn — retention still matters, just don't expect exclusivity.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
