# Earn the right to persuade by replaying the audience’s pressures and using shared identity.

## R — Reading

> “People love to have their own views replayed. We have a deep need to be heard and understood, particularly when that understanding comes from someone we admire and respect – a leader.”
- **Atomic executable skill:** Start a contentious message by naming the audience’s feelings and pressures in their words, then shift from “you” to “we.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Audience is angry, excluded, anxious, or likely to see the speaker as against them.
  - **Intervene:** Use “I know…”/“We know…” playback for concerns, then first-person plural for the shared path.
  - **Assess:** Hostility drops enough for the audience to hear the substantive case.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Nick Clegg aligned with viewers in televised debates by showing he understood how they felt.
  2. Obama uses “we/us/our” more than “I/me/mine,” strengthening shared identity.
- **Trigger phrases:** “They won’t listen”; “stakeholders are hostile”; “we need buy-in”; “you all have to…”
- **Sibling confusion risks:** Not flattery; not apology theater; not mere active listening without persuasion.
- **Boundaries:** Do not claim feelings you have not checked; avoid manipulative false solidarity.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Count audience-feeling phrases and “we” pronouns.
  - **V2:** Ask audience if they felt understood.
  - **V3:** Track reduced interruptions, objections, or escalation.”
