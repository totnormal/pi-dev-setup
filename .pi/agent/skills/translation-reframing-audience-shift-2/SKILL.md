## When to Use
Use when translating core marketing, tech, business prose, or highly personal essays, where preserving character, wit, rhythm, and native-speaker immersion is infinitely more important than word-for-word accuracy.

## Procedure
1. Step 1: Analyze both source/target audiences (expertise, goals, context, constraints).
2. Step 2: Establish localization constraints (formal vs. informal, active vs. passive, technical vs. business).
3. Step 3: Map English nouns and metaphors to native target-language idioms. If the original imagery is secondary to the point, transpose it to a culturally natural equivalent (The 'Gogoașă' Principle).
4. Step 4: Transpose standard technical/marketing borrowings based on professional norms (leaving consecrated terms untouched).
5. Step 5: Adjust narrator cadence using active-voice verbs, dramatic punctuation, and optional punchy sentences to control reading speed.
6. Step 6: Validate emotional weight and natural flow against the localization rubric.

## Pitfalls
- Literal metaphor-calquing (e.g., translating a source food metaphor that reads awkwardly in the target language).
- Stiff passive structures (which destroy conversational energy and narrator voice).
- Literal translations of vulgarity or hyperbole that feel syntactically awkward or mismatched in register.
- Failing to adjust spatial/physical descriptions to match real-world physical logic rather than textual literalism.

## Verification
1. Target text reads as though it were originally conceived in the target language.
2. Narrator voice has identical personality and pacing to the source.
3. All technical/marketing definitions retain their exact business/subtle meaning (e.g., effectiveness vs. efficiency).
