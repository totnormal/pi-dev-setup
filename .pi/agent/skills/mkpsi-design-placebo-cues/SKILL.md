# Use benign placebo cues to change felt efficacy or confidence.

## R — Reading

> “For something to be effective as a self-administered drug, it has to involve an element of illogicality, waste, unpleasantness, effort or costliness.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Desired state is partly automatic: confidence, recovery, bravery, energy, calm, patience, perceived potency.
  - **Intervene:** Add cues of potency: price, ritual, scarcity, restriction, distinctive taste, specialized packaging, color, professional care, or difficulty.
  - **Assess:** Measure subjective and behavioral effects while preserving truthful material claims.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Nurofen variants: condition-specific packaging, brand, price, and color can increase placebo power even with same active ingredient.
  2. Red Bull: high price, weird taste, small can, taurine rumors, and extreme-sport associations made it a potent commercial placebo.
- **Trigger phrases:** “It’s chemically identical”; “The packaging shouldn’t matter”; “Can we make it cheap, abundant, and pleasant?”; “People just need the active ingredient.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not medical fraud; placebo cues may supplement but not replace active treatment where efficacy matters.
  - Not sensory branding alone; cues must imply the intended automatic state.
- **Boundaries:** Require ethics review for health, addiction, children, financial vulnerability, or risk-taking contexts.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Compare identical functional product with different placebo cues.
  - **V2:** Separate expectation effects from real functional effects; watch for risk compensation.
  - **V3:** Validate long-term trust: users should not feel cheated when they learn how cues work.”
