# Start with Best-Fit Customers

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Your best-fit customers hold the key to understanding what your product is.
>
> — April Dunford, Step 1: Understand the Customers Who Love Your Product

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Instead of averaging all customers, Dunford starts from the customers who understood quickly, bought quickly, rarely discounted, retained, referred, and became fans. These customers reveal which value is strongest and which characteristics make buyers care. Positioning should first serve the near-term segment you can win, not the broadest imaginable market.

---

## A1 — Application in the Book (Past Application)

Dunford describes finding clearer patterns when surveying ecstatic customers rather than the entire base. In the database case, “companies with lots of data” was too broad; banks with time-sensitive analysis needs cared much more about fast answers.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - List customers who bought quickly, use the product deeply, renew, refer, or act as references.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

2. **Extract shared characteristics beyond demographics**
   - Extract shared characteristics beyond demographics: workflow, urgency, current tools, constraints, business trigger, and success metric.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

3. **Step 3**
   - Map which differentiated value each group cares about most and why.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

4. **Step 4**
   - Choose the narrowest segment that can meet near-term revenue goals.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

5. **Verification layer**
   - Verification layer: reject segments that are not identifiable in a prospect list or do not have a specific unmet need tied to your value.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not use “everyone can benefit” as a target market.
- Do not overfit to one noisy customer unless there are patterns across multiple best-fit customers.
- Do not use for investor TAM storytelling; this is customer-facing positioning.

### Common failure modes from Dunford's books

- Treating positioning as copywriting rather than strategic context.
- Talking about features before clarifying alternatives and value.
- Using generic trends, generic problems, or generic proof.
- Letting the buyer infer the purchase criteria alone.
