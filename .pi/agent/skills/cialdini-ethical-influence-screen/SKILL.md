# Ethical Influence Screen

## R — Reading

> “Those who use the influence process dishonestly will lose the most important of all their resources: their reputation.”
>
> — Robert B. Cialdini, Influence epilogue; Pre-Suasion ch.13

---

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

1. Ethical influence is not weaker influence; it is influence constrained by truth, autonomy, and long-term trust.
2. A tactic is acceptable only when the evidence is real, the offer can genuinely help, and the audience keeps a fair ability to decline.
3. The screen should be applied before copy, UX, sales scripts, or campaigns are improved.
4. If a tactic requires fabrication, concealment, artificial pressure, or exploitation of vulnerability, reject it and redesign the offer/context.

---

## A1 — Application in the source

### Case 1: Cialdini’s “detective of influence” warning
- **Problem**: Influence professionals can exploit automatic shortcuts.
- **Methodology use**: Cialdini repeatedly separates legitimate evidence from counterfeit triggers.
- **Result**: The agent should validate factual basis before using a lever.

### Case 2: Pre-Suasion ethical-use chapter
- **Problem**: Pre-suasive contexts can shift attention before people notice.
- **Methodology use**: Because the effect is subtle, the ethical bar is higher.
- **Result**: Use context-setting only when it prepares people to evaluate relevant truths.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not launder coercive goals into “ethical framing.”
- Do not create fake scarcity, fake authority, fake reviews, fake unity, or hidden opt-outs.
- If the audience includes vulnerable people or high-stakes domains, raise the evidentiary and disclosure bar.

### Global Cialdini safety constraints

- Truthful evidence beats persuasion technique; if the evidence is weak, say so.
- Preserve meaningful choice; avoid hidden defaults, fake constraints, or social pressure that makes refusal unsafe.
- Name the influence mechanism in the answer so the user can judge it consciously.
