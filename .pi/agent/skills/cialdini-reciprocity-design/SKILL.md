# Reciprocity Design

## R — Reading

> “The rule says that we should try to repay, in kind, what another person has provided us.”
>
> — Robert B. Cialdini, Chapter 2: Reciprocation

---

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

1. Reciprocity works because people feel a social obligation to return genuine favors, gifts, concessions, and helpfulness.
2. For ethical use, the first move must have stand-alone value and must not be disguised debt.
3. There are two main designs: value-first reciprocity (help/sample/insight before ask) and reciprocal concession (make a larger request, retreat to a fair smaller one).
4. The request should be proportional, transparent, and easy to refuse.

---

## A1 — Application in the source

### Case 1: Hare Krishna flower gift
- **Problem**: A small unsolicited “gift” increased donations.
- **Methodology use**: The flower created obligation even when recipients disliked it.
- **Result**: Final skill must avoid unwanted gift-pressure and make acceptance optional.

### Case 2: Boy Scout tickets/chocolate bar
- **Problem**: A rejected expensive ticket was followed by a smaller candy request.
- **Methodology use**: The concession made the smaller purchase feel reciprocal.
- **Result**: Use concession ladders only when both asks are honest and not inflated traps.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Never use unwanted gifts to create psychological debt.
- Avoid in high-power-imbalance settings where refusal is costly.
- Do not make the first ask artificially extreme just to manipulate contrast.

### Global Cialdini safety constraints

- Truthful evidence beats persuasion technique; if the evidence is weak, say so.
- Preserve meaningful choice; avoid hidden defaults, fake constraints, or social pressure that makes refusal unsafe.
- Name the influence mechanism in the answer so the user can judge it consciously.
