# Choose the psychologically richest moment to depict — not the moment of peak performance, but the moment closest to the human heart.

## R — Reading

> "The moment that convinces and sells is not necessarily the one that shows the product at peak performance. It is the one closer to the human heart — the moment of sudden recognition: "Gee, that's true.""
>
> — Source: Ch. 8 "The Psycho-Economic Age" — "The Moment That Sells"

**Source mechanism:** - **Recognize:** Buying is a continuous drama with many possible moments to depict. The "obvious" moment (peak performance, finished product) is usually NOT the most persuasive.
- **Interpret:** The psychologically pregnant moment triggers projection, identification, and emotional rehearsal of the purchase. Three criteria: (1) projection into the future, (2) insight into the past, (3) sudden recognition ("aha — that's me").
- **Act:** Survey all possible moments in the buying/usage drama. Test each against the three criteria. Choose the moment that triggers "I see myself there," not the moment that shows the product at its best.

**Evidence type:** source-specific candidate extraction; evidence grade must still be checked before rollout.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not manipulate emotions dishonestly — the depicted moment must be authentic to the actual experience.
- Do not use anxiety-based moments manipulatively (fear appeals have ethical limits).
- Some products (commodities, B2B inputs) may not have emotionally pregnant moments — functional messaging may be appropriate.
- The user only wants a book summary or author biography.
- The artifact affects high-risk contexts — minors, addiction, debt/financial hardship, health/legal/financial decisions, crisis/mental health, political persuasion, employment/housing/credit eligibility, or consent/privacy flows — unless the intervention clearly advances user welfare and preserves informed choice.

### Source-specific misuse risks

- The "pregnant moment" concept is rooted in Lessing's Laocoön and classical art theory; modern short-form digital media needs faster emotional hooks.
- What triggers "aha" varies by culture, generation, and context — validate with the specific audience.
