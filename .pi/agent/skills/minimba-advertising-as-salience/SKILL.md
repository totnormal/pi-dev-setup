# Advertising As Salience

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> The shift in thinking: from Unique Selling Proposition and Persuasion to Getting noticed, Refreshing and building memory structures, and Salience.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010), Ch 9 How Advertising Really Works; Ch 12 Seven Rules for Growing Brands

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Advertising works primarily by creating and refreshing memory structures (mental availability), not by persuading consumers with rational arguments. Effects are spread over time — today's ad affects a purchase months from now. The primary role is maintenance (preventing decline), not growth spikes. Advertising's sales effects are hard to see in aggregate data because they are layered thinly over many future purchases. Reach all category buyers (especially light/non-buyers), not just heavy buyers.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Sharp's "Georgiana and June" lemonade parable shows advertising gradually builds share without any visible "jump". Single-source data (linking individual ad exposure to individual purchases) reveals advertising effects masked in aggregate sales data. The airplane analogy: "The sales of a brand are like the height at which an airplane flies. Advertising spend is like its engines: while the engines are running everything is fine, but when the engines stop, the descent eventually starts."

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

This model applies to brand advertising for established products. Direct-response advertising, time-sensitive offers ("sale ends Thursday"), and new-product launches DO have immediate effects. Do not use this model to justify cutting all measurable objectives — mental availability can and should be tracked.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
