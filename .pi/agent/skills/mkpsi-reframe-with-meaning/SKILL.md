# Create value by changing meaning, not the object.

## R — Reading

> “We don’t value things; we value their meaning. What they are is determined by the laws of physics, but what they mean is determined by the laws of psychology.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** The product/service is treated as a commodity or low-status object despite objective usefulness.
  - **Intervene:** Attach meaning via provenance, story, scarcity, ritual, honor, contribution, or identity.
  - **Assess:** Measure willingness to pay, adoption, pride of display, and word-of-mouth before changing the underlying product.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Prussian iron jewellery: “I gave gold for iron” made iron replicas higher status than gold because they signaled generosity and patriotism.
  2. Marilyn Monroe coin exercise: a normal coin becomes more valuable by gaining provenance.
- **Trigger phrases:** “It’s just a commodity”; “We need to improve the actual thing”; “There’s no value unless we change the product”; “People only care about specs.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not deception; meaning must not falsify material claims.
  - Not premiumization alone; the skill is psychological value creation, which can also make public goods feel fairer.
- **Boundaries:** Do not use to hide defects, inflate essential goods exploitatively, or make false provenance claims.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Test two meaning frames against a plain control.
  - **V2:** Separate material quality effects from narrative/identity effects.
  - **V3:** Track durability: does the meaning survive repeated use and social scrutiny?”

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

- **Recognize:** The product/service is treated as a commodity or low-status object despite objective usefulness.
  - **Intervene:** Attach meaning via provenance, story, scarcity, ritual, honor, contribution, or identity.

  - **V3:** Track durability: does the meaning survive repeated use and social scrutiny?
