# Branded House Strategy (+ Different-Identities nuance)

## R — Reading

> "The branded house option ... puts a lot of eggs in one basket. ... the branded house enhances clarity, synergy, and leverage and thus should be the default brand architecture option. Any other strategy requires compelling reasons."
>
> — Aaker & Joachimsthaler (2000), p. 15

> "There usually needs to be a limited number of identities that share common elements but that have distinctions as well. For example, GE Capital requires certain associations that are inappropriate for GE appliances."
>
> — Aaker & Joachimsthaler (2000), p. 16–17

---

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

**Branded House** = a single master brand spans many offerings; subbrands become *descriptive* (Virgin Rail, Virgin Cola; Sony; Caterpillar; Healthy Choice; BMW). The master moves from primary to *dominant* driver.

Three payoffs make it the **default**:
1. **Clarity** — customers know exactly what's offered; one story is easier to understand/recall than a dozen.
2. **Synergy** — visibility/associations in one market help others (GE: perceived small-appliance leader years after exit; 80%+ recalled a GE Plastics ad that never ran).
3. **Leverage** — the master works harder across more contexts; aligns with business strategy of asset leverage.

**The nuance (avoid two errors):** (a) *identity anarchy* — dozens of unrelated identities under one name; (b) *single compromise* — one forced identity that's mediocre everywhere. The right answer: a *limited* set of identities sharing common elements but with distinctions (GE Capital vs GE Appliances).

Costs: concentration risk ("eggs in one basket" — Levi's/Nike/Kodak struggled under wide umbrellas), and less ability to target sharply.

---

## A1 — Application in the source

### Case 1: Virgin
- **Use**: one master across Airlines/Express/Radio/Rail/Cola/Jeans/Music.
- **Result**: clarity (service, innovation, fun, value, underdog) + synergy + leverage.

### Case 2: GE synergy
- **Use**: large-appliance ads buoyed small-appliance perception; cross-unit ad exposure accumulated.
- **Result**: brand exposure impact "far beyond intended function."

### Case 3: GE Capital vs GE Appliances
- **Use**: same master, *different* (limited) identities with shared + distinct elements.
- **Result**: avoids both anarchy and mediocre compromise.

## A2 — Future Trigger

1. A portfolio naturally shares one promise and can run under one master.
2. Wanting maximum clarity/synergy/leverage and the master has strong equity.
3. Managing one master across segments/countries needing a *few* (not one, not many) identities.

### Language signals
- "brand everything under [master]"
- "umbrella brand like Virgin/Sony"
- "one story across the portfolio"
- "leverage [master] everywhere"

### Distinction from siblings
- vs `aaker-relation-house-of-brands`: opposite end (one master vs independent brands).
- vs `aaker-relation-subbrand-codriver`: subbrand keeps a named driver entity; branded-house reduces it to a descriptor.

---

## E — Execution steps

1. **Confirm master equity** — is the master strong/credible enough to carry the offerings?
   - Done when: yes/no with the master's core associations listed.
2. **Default to branded house** unless a compelling separation reason exists (see aaker-relation-select-separation).
3. **Design the identity set** — define a *limited* number of identities sharing common elements but distinct where needed (GE Capital vs Appliances).
4. **Use descriptors, not free subbrands**, for variations within one identity.
5. **Watch concentration risk** — monitor for umbrella strain (Levi's/Nike/Kodak lessons).

---

## B — Boundary

### Don't use for
- Offerings whose associations *conflict* with the master (use house-of-brands/endorsed).
- When a separate brand is "absolutely necessary" (see aaker-relation-select-separation / pitfalls).

### Failure modes warned by the authors
- Identity anarchy (too many identities) and single-mediocre-compromise (two errors to avoid).
- Over-wide umbrella straining a cool/quality image (Levi's, Nike, Kodak).

### Author blind spots
- Digital/owned-media reduces the "egg in one basket" risk via precise targeting under one master.

### Easily confused with
- `aaker-relation-house-of-brands` (opposite end) and `aaker-relation-subbrand-codriver`.
