# Commitment Design

## R — Reading

> “Once we have made a choice or taken a stand, we will encounter personal and interpersonal pressures to behave consistently with that commitment.”
>
> — Robert B. Cialdini, Influence ch.3; Pre-Suasion ch.14

---

## I — Methodology skeleton (Interpretation)

1. People want to act consistently with choices they have made, especially when commitments are active, public, effortful, and internally owned.
2. The ethical version asks for small commitments that serve the user’s own stated goal.
3. Durability comes from converting a temporary yes into a concrete next action, written plan, calendar event, public promise, or identity-consistent pledge.
4. The agent must distinguish supportive commitment design from exploitative lock-in.

---

## A1 — Application in the source

### Case 1: Racetrack bettors
- **Problem**: Bettors became more confident immediately after placing a bet.
- **Methodology use**: The act of commitment changed their interpretation of the same evidence.
- **Result**: Small choices alter future belief and behavior.

### Case 2: Medical appointment write-down
- **Problem**: Patients who wrote appointment details themselves were less likely to no-show.
- **Methodology use**: An active written commitment created follow-through.
- **Result**: Design should ask users to author their next step.

## E — Execution steps

1. **Clarify the audience’s own goal; do not impose a commitment that serves only the seller.**
   - Done when: the output names the evidence, the intended behavioral effect, and the ethical constraint.
2. **Ask for the smallest active commitment that predicts the desired next action.**
   - Done when: the output names the evidence, the intended behavioral effect, and the ethical constraint.
3. **Make it concrete: written, scheduled, public when appropriate, and effortful enough to matter.**

4. **Add a post-suasion lock: reminder, implementation intention, or cue tied to the commitment.**

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not use for hidden lock-in, forced continuity, cancellation friction, or sunk-cost traps.
- Avoid public commitments where embarrassment or reputational pressure would be disproportionate.
- If motivation is absent, return to value/problem diagnosis instead of forcing commitment.

### Global Cialdini safety constraints

- Truthful evidence beats persuasion technique; if the evidence is weak, say so.
- Preserve meaningful choice; avoid hidden defaults, fake constraints, or social pressure that makes refusal unsafe.
- Name the influence mechanism in the answer so the user can judge it consciously.
