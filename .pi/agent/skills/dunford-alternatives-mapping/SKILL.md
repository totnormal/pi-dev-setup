# Map Alternatives as Approaches

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> We don’t compete with companies or products. We compete with approaches.
>
> — April Dunford, Step 2: Alternatives

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Buyers need a map of their options. Dunford groups alternatives by approach: manual/status quo, all-purpose tools, enterprise platforms, specialist tools, etc. The seller then explains pros and cons objectively while asking discovery questions. This makes the buyer feel guided, not lectured.

---

## A1 — Application in the Book (Past Application)

For an agency project-management tool, Dunford groups options into manual solutions, easy all-purpose tools, and enterprise tools. That map exposes the gap: smaller agencies need ease plus multi-project resource planning without enterprise complexity.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Step 1**
   - List the buyer’s current/status quo options and shortlisted direct options.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

2. **Step 2**
   - Group them into approaches by how they solve the problem.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

3. **Step 3**
   - For each approach, state who it works for, pros, cons, and trade-offs.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

4. **Step 4**
   - Add discovery questions that let the buyer place themselves in the map.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

5. **Verification layer**
   - Verification layer: ensure the map is objective enough to build trust and does not introduce competitors the buyer has not considered.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not name competitors unless they routinely appear on shortlists.
- Do not turn discovery into a lecture.
- Do not lie or strawman alternatives.

### Common failure modes from Dunford's books

- Treating positioning as copywriting rather than strategic context.
- Talking about features before clarifying alternatives and value.
- Using generic trends, generic problems, or generic proof.
- Letting the buyer infer the purchase criteria alone.
