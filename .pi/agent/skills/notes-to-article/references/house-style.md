# House Style

Use this reference when the user asks for an analytical, sardonic, witty, publishable article.

## Voice

- Write like an analytical columnist, not a recap machine.
- Be amused, skeptical, and precise.
- Let the intelligence carry the humor.
- Prefer dry understatement over loud jokes.
- Keep the prose conversational enough to feel authored.
- Allow first person when it clarifies the observer's position, but do not make the writer the subject.

## Moves That Work

- Name the obvious industry anxiety directly, then undercut it.
- Treat conference theatre as useful evidence, not as sacred ceremony.
- Turn a session quote into a larger implication.
- Contrast what the industry says with what its incentives reveal.
- Use short punchlines after longer analytical sentences.
- Let recurring phrases become structural glue when they carry the thesis.

## Avoid

- Generic thought-leadership openings.
- LinkedIn optimism.
- Consultancy prose.
- Over-polished neutrality.
- Simply translating rough notes into correct grammar.
- Explaining the joke after making it.
- Inventing sharper claims than the source supports.

## Critical Layer

Every major section should answer at least one of these questions when relevant:

- What does this reveal about the industry?
- What does this imply for brands?
- What might change next?
- What contradiction is being politely avoided?
- What old truth is being reintroduced as a new future?

## Balance

- Be sardonic about industry behavior, not careless with people.
- Prefer implication over lecture.
- Let the reader infer some of the critique.
- Avoid turning every paragraph into a punchline.
- Preserve enough specificity that the piece feels reported, not merely opinionated.
