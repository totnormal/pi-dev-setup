# Transformation Pattern

Use this reference when turning rough notes into a publishable article.

## Source Inputs

Rough notes may contain:

- Proposed title
- Intro fragments
- Personal comments
- Transcript excerpts
- Speaker names and session titles
- Quotes
- Statistics
- Half-formed arguments
- Repeated or misspelled phrases
- Conclusion notes

Do not preserve the rough order by default. Preserve the meaning, facts, and best authorial intent.

## Conversion Pattern

1. Find the strongest thesis hiding across the notes.
2. Identify which sessions or examples prove that thesis.
3. Group related material into sections.
4. Decide what to cut, merge, or compress.
5. Preserve distinctive phrases or personal comments that can become the article's voice.
6. Add critical interpretation and subtle implications.
7. Write a coherent article with a clear opening, argument, section flow, and ending.
8. Re-read the draft against the source and remove unsupported flourish.

## Useful Article Shape

- Title with a clear subject and tonal edge.
- Standfirst or opening paragraph that names the tension.
- Byline if the source includes one or the user requests it.
- Intro that turns the messy notes into a thesis.
- 4-6 named sections.
- Evidence from notes inside each section.
- Critical implication woven into each major section.
- Conclusion that returns to the thesis with a human or strategic insight.

## Compression

Compress aggressively. A strong article should not carry every note forward.

Keep:

- Facts that support the thesis.
- Quotes that crystallize an argument.
- Statistics that change the stakes.
- Specific examples that prevent abstraction.
- Personal comments that sharpen the author's angle.

Cut or merge:

- Repeated transcript fragments.
- Administrative session details.
- Quotes that repeat the surrounding prose.
- Interesting but thesis-adjacent material.
- Generic claims that could appear in any industry article.

## Fact Handling

- Keep names, titles, brands, dates, awards, figures, and quoted claims faithful to the source.
- If a source note is garbled but the intended meaning is clear, rewrite the meaning cleanly without pretending exact wording is a quote.
- Use direct quotation marks only for text that appears as a quote in the notes.
- When uncertain, attribute generally rather than inventing precision.
