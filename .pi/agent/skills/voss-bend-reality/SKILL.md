# Bend Their Reality Without Splitting the Difference

## R — 原文 (Reading)

> “We don’t compromise because it’s right; we compromise because it is easy and because it saves face.”
>
> — Chris Voss, Chapter 6 — Bend Their Reality

---

## I — 方法论骨架 (Interpretation)

Compromise is often a lazy midpoint that satisfies neither side. Voss uses behavioral framing: deadlines are usually less absolute than they feel; people fear losses more than equivalent gains; “fair” is emotionally loaded; anchors shape expectations; non-cash terms can create value. The goal is not to bully but to reshape the counterpart’s perception of what accepting or rejecting means.

## A2 — 触发场景 (Future Trigger) ★

### 用户会在什么情境下需要这个 skill?

1. The user is about to meet halfway despite a bad outcome.
2. The other side invokes fairness, deadline, or a high anchor.
3. The user needs to frame a proposal so refusal feels like a loss.

### 语言信号 (用户的话里出现这些就应激活)

- "split the difference"
- "is this fair"
- "they gave me a deadline"
- "how do I frame my offer"

## B — 边界 (Boundary) ★

### 不要在以下情况使用此 skill

- Do not ignore truly hard deadlines or legal constraints.
- Do not weaponize “fair” dishonestly.
- If the dispute is about values or safety, midpoint avoidance may not solve it.

### 作者在书中警告的失败模式

- 不要把谈判当成战斗、辩论或单纯的价格拉扯; 先发现真实约束。
- 不要追逐表面 “Yes”; 没有 “How” 的 yes 可能毫无执行力。

### 作者的盲点 / 时代局限

- 本书来自 FBI/危机谈判背景, 现实商务/亲密关系中权力、法律和长期关系约束更复杂。
- 书中战术很强, 但若脱离伦理边界, 容易滑向操控。

### 容易混淆的邻近方法论

- Use voss-ackerman-bargaining for the final numeric haggle.
- Use voss-black-swan-leverage when hidden information may change leverage entirely.
