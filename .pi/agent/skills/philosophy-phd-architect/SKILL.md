## Dissertation Baseline

The master's dissertation argues that classical in-depth interviews / semi-structured individual interviews in marketing often produce coherent but superficial self-presentations and rationalized preferences. It proposes that tools inspired by philosophical counselling can deepen consumer research by accessing latent convictions, worldview structures, practical reasoning, narrative identity, and symbolic meaning.

Main conceptual pillars from the dissertation:
- **Bourdieu**: habitus, taste, field, symbolic capital, illusio, distinction, symbolic violence;
- **Baudrillard**: sign, simulacrum, hyperreality, loss of referent;
- **Debord**: spectacle, social relation mediated by images, contemporary consumer performance;
- **Ricoeur**: narrative identity, idem/ipse, emplotment;
- **Belk**: possessions and the extended self;
- **Lahav / philosophical counselling**: worldview interpretation;
- **Elliot D. Cohen / LBT**: practical syllogisms, implicit evaluative premises, logical-emotional structures;
- **Hoogendijk**: philosopher in business as vision developer.

The dissertation's applied thesis: a brand may be analysed simultaneously as:
1. **obiect social** structured by habitus and field;
2. **semn** circulating in regimes of simulacra/spectacle;
3. **suport identitar** integrated into narrative identity and extended self.

## Candidate PhD Directions to Explore

Treat these as starting hypotheses, not fixed decisions:

### A. Philosophical counselling as a method for investigating consumer preferences
Question: How can philosophical counselling deepen qualitative consumer research beyond declarative preferences?
Possible contribution: an epistemological and methodological account of philosophical counselling as interpretive consumer inquiry.
Risks: must avoid becoming merely a marketing technique; needs clear philosophical stakes and empirical protocol.

### B. A philosophical-counselling protocol for brand development
Question: Can a replicable philosophical counselling protocol help build brands by clarifying worldview, values, contradictions, and narrative identity?
Possible contribution: applied-philosophy method for ethical brand formation.
Risks: must define boundaries, consent, non-therapy, anti-manipulation, and validation.

### C. Limits of marketing through habitus, field, and illusio
Question: What are the structural limits of persuasion in branding, given habitus and illusio?
Possible contribution: philosophical critique of marketing's fantasy of unlimited influence; explains why some audiences cannot be converted unless the brand interacts with field-level stakes or shared illusio.
Risks: requires stronger Bourdieu scholarship and precise distinction between persuasion, resonance, conversion, and symbolic legitimacy.

### D. Brands, intimacy, and identity beyond Belk
Question: Can brands become part of a consumer's inner circle or identity, rather than merely extensions of personality/possessions?
Possible sources: Debord, Baudrillard, Belk, Robin Dunbar / Dunbar's number, identity theory, political ideology, consumption ideology.
Possible contribution: theory of brands as quasi-intimate symbolic agents or identity-membership structures.
Risks: high originality but also high speculative risk; needs strong conceptual controls and empirical/phenomenological grounding.

- **tema cu cel mai mare risc**.

## Ethical Guardrails

Because the project intersects with marketing, always check:
- Does the method clarify or manipulate?
- Is the respondent treated as a person, not merely an insight-source?
- Is the boundary with therapy explicit?
- Are consent, anonymization, and non-diagnosis addressed?
- Does the proposed brand application respect autonomy?
- Could the method increase exploitative persuasive power?

If the work risks becoming instrumental or manipulative, flag it and propose an ethical reformulation.
