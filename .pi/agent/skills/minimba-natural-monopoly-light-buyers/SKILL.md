# deciding who to target for growth — heavy "super consumers" vs light/non-buyers — or evaluating an acquisition strategy focused on existing heavy category buyers

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Larger brands tend to have proportionately more light category buyers in their user bases. Light, occasional buyers favour the bigger brands. This is the Natural Monopoly Law.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010); Marketing TEP — Byron Sharp, Ch 7 Passionate Customer Commitment; Ch 13 The Scientific Laws

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Big brands have a "monopoly" on light category buyers — not because light buyers prefer big brands, but as a statistical inevitability. If you buy only one soft drink this year, odds are it's Coca-Cola. This means targeting heavy "super consumers" is targeting the wrong people for growth — heavy buyers already have established repertoires and are fewer in number. Growth comes from recruiting the numerous light/non-buyers who naturally gravitate to bigger brands.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Heinz (53% share) buyers purchase tomato sauce 4x/year; C&B sauce (1% share) buyers purchase 8x/year — twice as often. The smaller brand's customers are heavier category buyers. Walkers crisps (68% share) has 37% 100%-loyal buyers because many are light category buyers who only bought once.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Heavy buyers ARE valuable for feedback, word-of-mouth, and early adoption of innovations. The law says they're not the primary growth ENGINE, not that they're worthless. New/niche brands may rationally target heavy buyers initially before broadening.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
