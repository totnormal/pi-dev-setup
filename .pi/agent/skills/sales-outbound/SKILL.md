# Section 1: Cold Email Writing

## Principles

### Write like a peer, not a vendor
Sound like a sharp colleague who noticed something relevant — not a sales machine.
Use contractions. Read it aloud. If it sounds like marketing copy, rewrite it.

### Every sentence must earn its place
Cold email is ruthlessly short. If a sentence doesn't move the reader toward
replying, cut it.

### Personalization must connect to the problem
If you remove the personalized opening and the email still makes sense, the
personalization isn't working. The observation should naturally lead to why
you're reaching out.

### Lead with their world, not yours
"You/your" should dominate over "I/we." Never open with who you are or what
your company does.

### One ask, low friction
Interest-based CTAs ("Worth exploring?" / "Would this be useful?") beat meeting
requests. One CTA per email. Make it easy to say yes with a one-line reply.

## Structure Frameworks

| Framework | Shape |
|-----------|-------|
| Observation | You noticed X → which means Y challenge → we helped Z with that → interested? |
| Question | Struggling with X? → We do Y → Company Z saw [result] → worth a look? |
| Trigger | Congrats on X → that usually creates Y challenge → we've helped similar companies → curious? |
| Story | [Similar company] had [problem] → solved it this way → relevant to you? |

## Subject Lines
- 2-4 words, lowercase, no punctuation tricks
- Internal-looking: "reply rates", "hiring ops", "Q2 forecast"
- No product pitches, no urgency, no emojis, no prospect's name

## Follow-Up Sequences (Cold Outreach)
- 3-5 total emails with increasing gaps
- Each adds something new — different angle, fresh proof, useful resource
- "Just checking in" gives no reason to respond
- Each email should stand alone (they may not have read the previous one)
- The breakup email is the last touch — honor it

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Sounds like a human wrote it (read aloud)
- [ ] Personalization connected to the problem
- [ ] One clear, low-friction ask
- [ ] No jargon: "synergy", "leverage", "circle back", "best-in-class"
- [ ] No "I hope this email finds you well"
- [ ] No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subject lines
- [ ] One proof point beats ten features

# Section 2: Lifecycle Email Sequences

## Core Principles

### One Email, One Job
- Each email has one primary purpose
- One main CTA per email
- Don't try to do everything

### Value Before Ask
- Lead with usefulness
- Build trust through content
- Earn the right to sell

### Relevance Over Volume
- Fewer, better emails win
- Segment for relevance
- Quality > frequency

## Sequence Types

### Welcome Sequence (Post-Signup)
**5-7 emails over 12-14 days**
1. Welcome + deliver promised value (immediate)
2. Quick win (day 1-2)
3. Story/Why (day 3-4)
4. Social proof (day 5-6)
5. Overcome objection (day 7-8)
6. Core feature highlight (day 9-11)
7. Conversion (day 12-14)

### Lead Nurture Sequence
**6-8 emails over 2-3 weeks**
1. Deliver lead magnet + intro (immediate)
2. Expand on topic (day 2-3)
3. Problem deep-dive (day 4-5)
4. Solution framework (day 6-8)
5. Case study (day 9-11)
6. Differentiation (day 12-14)
7. Objection handler (day 15-18)
8. Direct offer (day 19-21)

### Re-Engagement Sequence
**3-4 emails over 2 weeks, triggered by 30-60 days inactivity**
1. Check-in (genuine concern)
2. Value reminder (what's new)
3. Incentive (special offer)
4. Last chance (stay or unsubscribe)

### Onboarding Sequence (Product Users)
**5-7 emails over 14 days**
1. Welcome + first step (immediate)
2. Getting started help (day 1)
3. Feature highlight (day 2-3)
4. Success story (day 4-5)
5. Check-in (day 7)
6. Advanced tip (day 10-12)
7. Upgrade/expand (day 14+)

## Timing Rules
- Welcome email: immediately
- Early sequence: 1-2 days apart
- Nurture: 2-4 days apart
- Long-term: weekly or bi-weekly
- B2B: avoid weekends; B2C: test weekends

## Email Copy Structure
1. **Hook**: First line grabs attention
2. **Context**: Why this matters to them
3. **Value**: The useful content
4. **CTA**: What to do next
5. **Sign-off**: Human, warm close

## Subject Line Patterns (Lifecycle)
- Question: "Still struggling with X?"
- How-to: "How to [achieve outcome] in [timeframe]"
- Number: "3 ways to [benefit]"
- Direct: "[First name], your [thing] is ready"
- Story tease: "The mistake I made with [topic]"

## Email Length
- 50-125 words: transactional
- 150-300 words: educational
- 300-500 words: story-driven

# Section 3: Multi-Touch Outbound Sequences

## Sequence Types
1. **Classic Cold Outreach** (7 emails, 2 weeks)
2. **Fast-Track** (5 emails, 1 week)
3. **Long-Play Nurture** (12-14 emails, 4-6 weeks)
4. **Event/Trigger-Based** (3-5 emails, event-specific)
5. **Re-Engagement** (5 emails, revive old leads)

## Personalization Levels
- **Hyper-Personal**: Unique research per prospect
- **Account-Based**: Company-specific messaging
- **Segment-Based**: Industry/role personalization
- **Volume**: Template with merge tags

## Channel-Specific Rules
- **Email**: Research-driven, value-first, soft CTA
- **Phone**: Reference email touch, add urgency, book meeting
- **LinkedIn**: Comment on their content first, then connect with personalized note
- **Video**: 60-second personalized Loom for high-value accounts

## A/B Testing Framework
- Test one variable at a time (subject line, CTA, personalization depth)
- Minimum 100 emails per variant for statistical significance
- Track: open rate, reply rate, positive reply rate, meeting booked rate

## Metrics to Track
| Metric | Good | Great |
|--------|------|-------|
| Open rate | 30-40% | 50%+ |
| Reply rate | 5-10% | 15%+ |
| Positive reply rate | 1-2% | 3%+ |
| Meeting booked rate | 1% | 2-3% |

# Section 4: SDR Prospecting Playbook

## Daily Activity Targets
- New accounts researched: 15-25
- Personalized emails sent: 30-50
- Follow-ups sent: 20-30
- Cold calls: 30-50
- LinkedIn connections: 15-25
- Meetings booked: 2-5

## Qualification Frameworks

### BANT
- **Budget**: Can they afford it?
- **Authority**: Are you talking to the decision maker?
- **Need**: Do they have a real problem?
- **Timing**: Is there urgency?

### MEDDPICC
- **Metrics**: How will success be measured?
- **Economic buyer**: Who controls the budget?
- **Decision process**: How do they buy?
- **Decision criteria**: What are they evaluating?
- **Paper process**: Legal/procurement steps
- **Identified pain**: What problem are we solving?
- **Champion**: Who's selling internally?
- **Competition**: Who else are they evaluating?

## Cadence Design (Multi-channel)
```
Day 1: Email 1 (personalized)
Day 2: LinkedIn connect
Day 3: Email 2 (value proof)
Day 4: Phone call
Day 7: Email 3 (different angle)
Day 9: LinkedIn message
Day 11: Phone call
Day 14: Email 4 (social proof)
Day 21: Email 5 (breakup)
```
