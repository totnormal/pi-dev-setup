# interpreting brand health/image/equity survey results

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> Buyers of different brands express very similar attitudes about their respective brands — the "I love my Mum and you love yours" pattern. Larger brands score higher on surveys simply because they have more users.
>
> — Source: How Brands Grow — Byron Sharp (2010); Romaniuk, Sharp & Ehrenberg (2007), Ch 5 Our Buyers Are Different; Ch 8 Differentiation vs Distinctiveness; Ch 13

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Brand image differences are largely an artifact of usage, not real differentiation. Buyers know and say more about brands they use; they think little about brands they don't. So larger brands always score higher on attitude/perception surveys — not because they're perceived differently, but because they have more users who can respond. Once you remove the usage effect, rival brands are perceived in much the same way. This law undermines most brand health tracking and personality research.

---

## A1 — Application in the Source Material (Past Application)

Collins (2002) showed that once familiarity is controlled, FedEx, Nokia, Oracle etc. score nearly identically on "trusted," "efficient," "innovative." Romaniuk & Ehrenberg found brand personality attributes are held by only ~5% of consumers and weakly repeated on re-interview. The double jeopardy sub-pattern means smaller brands score slightly lower on every attribute — but this is statistical, not perceptual.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

Some perceptual differences ARE real and functional (American brands perceived as American, expensive brands as expensive). But exotic/emotional attributes rarely differentiate once usage is controlled. Do not use this to dismiss all customer feedback — functional complaints (quality, service) are valid.

### Common corpus-level failure modes

- Replacing strategy with goals, slogans, or wish lists.
- Treating a case study or correlation as universal proof.
- Over-optimizing short-term metrics while starving long-term brand effects.
- Over-targeting existing customers while ignoring reach, penetration, and availability.
- Producing a plan with no capabilities, systems, owners, or review cadence.
