# Audit and replace metaphors that steer instinctive interpretation.

## R — Reading

> “Metaphors pack enormous power into a tiny space. They plant ideas deep in the instinctive mind, where they take root and grow, spreading around, affecting the way people think, feel and act.”
- **Atomic executable skill:** Underline every metaphor in a draft, infer its hidden equation (`x = y`), then keep, replace, or remove it based on the image and behavior it invites.
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Draft contains “drive,” “front line,” “toolkit,” “battle,” “journey,” “engine,” “platform,” or mixed figurative language.
  - **Intervene:** Convert each metaphor to an explicit frame; choose one coherent frame that supports the intended feeling/action.
  - **Assess:** Readers describe the desired image without naming conflicting images.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. “Arab Spring” implies natural renewal and inaction, unlike “tsunami,” “furnace,” or “disease.”
  2. Car metaphors make leaders feel like drivers but can make staff feel like disposable nuts and bolts.
- **Trigger phrases:** “Drive change”; “accelerate reform”; “fight the problem”; “front-line delivery”; “build momentum”; “unlock potential.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:** Overlaps with emotional word choice, but this skill is about image-frames, not isolated high-valence words.
- **Boundaries:** Avoid dehumanizing frames such as vermin/infestation; do not mix multiple frames in one strategic claim.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Count metaphors and list their frames.
  - **V2:** Ask listeners what mental picture they got.
  - **V3:** Compare action preferences after alternative metaphor versions.”
