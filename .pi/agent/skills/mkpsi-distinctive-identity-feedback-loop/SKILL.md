# Make the maker identifiable so quality and reputation can compound.

## R — Reading

> “Branding isn’t just something to add to great products – it’s essential to their existence.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Buyers cannot tell which version to trust, reward, avoid, or recommend.
  - **Intervene:** Create stable distinctive identity: name, mark, provenance, signature, recognizable form, or accountable maker.
  - **Assess:** Measure choice confidence, repeat purchase, quality improvement, and blame/reward precision.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. Hoverboards lacked a stable name/brand; battery fires contaminated the whole category and quality incentives collapsed.
  2. Soviet rivets and bread: anonymized production removed accountability until factories had to stamp names on rivets.
- **Trigger phrases:** “All suppliers look the same”; “Customers don’t know which one to choose”; “Bad actors hurt the whole category”; “It’s a race to the bottom.”
- **Sibling confusion risks:**
  - Not logo decoration; identity must enable feedback and reputation.
  - Not differentiation for novelty; distinctiveness must help buyers remember and sanction.
- **Boundaries:** Use where repeat exchange or word-of-mouth exists; weaker for anonymous one-shot transactions unless guarantees substitute.
- **Validation notes:**
  - **V1:** Test whether buyers can identify, recall, and distinguish the maker.
  - **V2:** Connect identity to quality claims, warranties, reviews, and aftercare.
  - **V3:** Track whether identity changes producer incentives and category trust over time.”
