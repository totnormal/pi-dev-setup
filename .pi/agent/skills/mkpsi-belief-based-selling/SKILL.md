# Sell by transmitting practiced belief, not pressure.

## R — Reading

> “Because, I realized, it wasn’t selling. I believed in running. I believed that if people got out and ran a few miles every day, the world would be a better place, and I believed these shoes were better to run in.”
- **RIA skeleton:**
  - **Recognize:** Conventional selling feels false because the seller is pushing product rather than expressing lived conviction.
  - **Intervene:** Go where believers gather, demonstrate the artifact in context, and speak from the practice’s values.
  - **Assess:** Measure voluntary pull: word-of-mouth visits, unsolicited calls, COD orders, and customers repeating the belief.
- **2 source examples:**
  1. After stores reject him, Knight drives to track meets, talks to coaches/runners/fans, and cannot write orders fast enough.
  2. Blue Ribbon’s early mail-order demand emerges because runners hear from other runners and seek out Tigers without formal sales infrastructure.

  - **V2:** Compare conversion from belief communities versus broad audiences.
  - **V3:** Codify the belief into training, copy, events, and customer stories without diluting authenticity.
- The user only wants a book summary or author biography.
