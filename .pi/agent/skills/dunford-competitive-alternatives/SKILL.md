# True Competitive Alternatives

## R — Original Passage (Reading)

> The competitive alternative is what your target customers would “use” or “do” if your product didn’t exist.
>
> — April Dunford, Obviously Awesome Step 4; Sales Pitch Step 2

---

## I — Methodology Skeleton (Interpretation)

Dunford’s alternative lens replaces company-centric competitor lists with customer-centric fallback behavior. Customers compare you against what they would actually do: another vendor, Excel, manual work, hiring someone, using a broader suite, or doing nothing. Your differentiation is only meaningful against that yardstick.

---

## A1 — Application in the Book (Past Application)

In Obviously Awesome, Dunford’s database customers did not name other databases as alternatives; they named BI tools and data warehouses. In Sales Pitch, she insists that “do nothing” and status quo solutions are real competitors because they often win when buyers lack confidence.

## E — Execution Steps

1. **Ask**
   - Ask: if we disappeared tomorrow, what would best-fit customers do instead?
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

2. **Step 2**
   - Separate alternatives into status quo, direct shortlist competitors, and indirect/manual approaches.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

3. **Step 3**
   - Cluster alternatives into approaches that share a logic, not just vendor names.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

4. **Step 4**
   - For each approach, list pros, cons, and the buyer type for whom it works best.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

5. **Verification layer**
   - Verification layer: check that alternatives reflect buyer behavior, not internal market maps; remove competitors buyers do not know or shortlist unless strategically necessary.
   - Completion standard: this step produces a concrete, inspectable note, list, decision, or revision.

## B — Boundary

### Do not use this skill when

- Do not introduce obscure competitors in sales materials if buyers have not considered them.
- Do not misrepresent competitors; the method relies on being a credible guide.
- Do not ignore the status quo just because it is not a product.

### Common failure modes from Dunford's books

- Treating positioning as copywriting rather than strategic context.
- Talking about features before clarifying alternatives and value.
- Using generic trends, generic problems, or generic proof.
- Letting the buyer infer the purchase criteria alone.
